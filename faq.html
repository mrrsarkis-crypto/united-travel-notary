Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before your mobile notary appointment.

What do I need to bring to my appointment?

A valid, unexpired government-issued photo ID (driver's license, passport, or state ID card) for each person signing — and your unsigned documents. Do not sign beforehand: acknowledgments and jurats require signing (or acknowledging) in the notary's presence.

What if my ID is expired?

California law requires current, unexpired ID (with limited exceptions for certain IDs expired less than 5 years with proper documentation). If your ID is expired, call us first at (424) 450-6387 — we can discuss whether credible witnesses or other options apply.

How long does an appointment take?

Most appointments take 15–30 minutes depending on the number of documents and signers. Loan signings typically run 30–45 minutes.

How much will it cost?

California caps notary fees at $15 per signature for acknowledgments and jurats. A travel fee applies for mobile visits and is always quoted upfront based on distance — you'll know your total before we arrive.

What areas do you serve?

We're based in Van Nuys and serve Los Angeles County — the San Fernando Valley, Burbank, Glendale, West Hollywood, and surrounding areas. Need us farther out? Ask — we travel by arrangement.

Do you offer evenings or weekends?

Yes. Appointments are available 7 days a week, including evenings, by arrangement. After-hours visits may include a surcharge, always quoted in advance.

Can you notarize at a hospital or nursing home?

Yes — bedside notarizations are one of our specialties. We'll coordinate with facility staff and family. The signer must be alert and able to communicate consent.

What payment methods do you accept?

Cash, major credit/debit cards, and electronic payment. Payment is due at the time of service.

Can a notary give me legal advice?

No. A California notary public is not an attorney and may not give legal advice, explain what your documents mean, or choose which notarial act you need beyond the certificate wording. For legal questions, consult a licensed attorney.

What is an apostille, and can you help?

An apostille is a state-issued authentication that makes a California document valid in another country. We prepare your documents correctly for the process and guide you through the Secretary of State submission.
